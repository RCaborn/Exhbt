# Do history undergraduates trust Wikipedia more than they admit?

BA History dissertation (extract), Year 3. Marlow University, April 2026. Supervisor: Dr A. Feld.

## Question

Lecturers tell history students not to cite Wikipedia. My question was how far students use it anyway, and whether those who use it most trust it most. My hypothesis was that heavy users would rate it as more reliable than light users.

## Method

I wrote a questionnaire of 12 questions and sent it to every history undergraduate at Marlow through the department mailing list in November 2025. 143 of 410 students replied. I also interviewed 8 of them for 20 minutes each and coded the transcripts by theme. I compared reliability ratings (1 to 5) between students who said they used Wikipedia weekly or more and those who used it less.

## Results

- Of those who replied, 87% said they had used Wikipedia for a university essay; 6% said they had cited it.
- Weekly users rated its reliability at a mean of 3.4 out of 5, against 2.6 for the rest.
- In interviews, 7 of 8 said they used it "to get the dates straight" before reading anything else.

## Conclusion

Students use Wikipedia as a first step far more than they cite it, and the hypothesis was supported: those who use it most rate it as more reliable. The gap between use and citation suggests the rule against citing it hides the habit without changing it.

## Limits

The students who replied chose to, so heavy users may be over- or under-counted, and all were at one university.
