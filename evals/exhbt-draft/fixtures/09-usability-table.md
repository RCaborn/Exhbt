# Usability test of the Hallmark Library booking page

HCI module coursework (COMP2410), Year 2, Northgate University. Spring term 2026.

## What I set out to find

Students keep complaining that booking a group study room on the library website is confusing. I wanted to find out which steps of the booking page people get stuck on, so the library's web team knows what to fix first.

## Method

I recruited five second-year students who had never booked a room. Each one did the same three tasks on a laptop while thinking aloud: find a free room for four people tomorrow afternoon, book it for two hours, and cancel the booking. I watched, took notes and timed nothing; the aim was to see where people hesitated, not how fast they were. After each session I sorted my notes into what happened at each task.

## Results

| Task | What happened | Main problem |
|---|---|---|
| Find a free room | All five found one, but only after scrolling past the floor plan | The list of free rooms sits below a large map |
| Book for two hours | Three of five booked one hour by mistake | The length menu defaults to one hour and is easy to miss |
| Cancel the booking | Two of five gave up | Cancel is under "My account", not on the booking itself |

Every participant said the confirmation email was clear.

## What I recommended

Move the list of free rooms above the map, make the length a required choice, and put a Cancel button on each booking. I sent these to the library web team as a one-page note.

## Reflection

Five people is enough to find the worst problems but not to say how common they are. Next time I would test the fixed page with a new group to check the changes work.
