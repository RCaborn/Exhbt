Does a 10-minute walk before a test improve recall in first-year students?

Exercise is known to lift mood. Whether a short walk helps recall straight after is less clear.

Hypothesis

Students who walk for 10 minutes before a word-list test will recall more words than students who sit.

Method

  1. Recruited 48 first-yearsvolunteers, two groups
  2. Walk or sit, 10 minutes
  3. Word-list recall test40 words
  4. Compared groups in Rt-test

Results

  • The walking group recalled 21.4 words on average; the sitting group 19.1.
  • The difference was significant, t(46) = 2.1, p = .04.
  • The gap was largest for words in the first half of the list.

Conclusion

A short walk before a test gave a small rise in recall, so the hypothesis was supported.

Limitations and next steps

One university and volunteers only, and the test came straight after. Next I'd test recall a day later.
