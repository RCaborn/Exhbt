# Making the site look and feel great: the plan

For Rory and Alex, 26 Nov 2026. You asked for Astryx as the design guide, more flair, a site where it's obvious what Exhbt does, and the ways other products draw people in and keep them going. We photographed 38 screens on a laptop and again at phone width, and six reviews went through them.

## What looks weak today

1. **Publishing an exhbt.** The biggest moment in the product is three lines in a blank white panel, ending on four equal buttons and a way back to a list.
2. **Home on a phone.** Words fill the first screen; the first picture of the product starts a screen and a half down, cut off before its flowchart. There are three ways in: "Build your roadmap", "Start with a free roadmap" and "Build your exhbt".
3. **Your exhbts.** Four rows carry twenty-four buttons, four pink Delete buttons shout louder than the one mint button, and each exhbt shows as a line of text.
4. **Account, Billing and job links.** Each page sets its own width, so the text's left edge jumps from screen to screen and never lines up with the logo, and forms float in shadowed boxes. This is Alex's "huge margins... block block".
5. **The exhbt editor on a phone.** Four strips above the page take well over a third of the screen, and "Review and publish" wraps onto two lines in its own button. The page itself is the best screen we have.
6. **The roadmap's result.** After a wait of up to a minute it opens on three rows of links in a mostly empty box, with the radar a click away.

## The direction

Build the whole app and every marketing page from Astryx, in Exhbt's colours and type, so every screen shares one layout, one set of buttons and fields, and one way of moving. Spend the character in a few places only: one loud line of type, the student's real work shown large, mint where it means something, and a finish worth sending. And make every screen say what it is for and what happens next, in the same words everywhere.

## Six things that make it Exhbt

1. **One loud line:** each screen has one line in the big heading type that nothing competes with, and a person's address is always set that way.
2. **Only the work is raised:** forms lie flat, and the only things with a frame and a shadow are pictures of real pages.
3. **Mint means go, live and checked:** one mint button a screen, always the next step, with Delete tucked in a menu that asks first.
4. **"It's live":** you publish, your tile turns from Draft to Live, and you get the link as it will look pasted into LinkedIn, with Copy link and one next step.
5. **An exhbt builds itself:** on Home the example assembles as you scroll to it, the question first, then the flowchart box by box, the graph and the ticks.
6. **The address becomes yours:** type your name on Home and the big address and the example page take it; scroll, and that page changes through the eight looks.

## How much show: three levels

- **Restrained.** "It's live", the example exhbt building itself on Home, and three small touches: the highlighter draws itself under the headline once, the radar draws itself once when a roadmap opens, and menus and panels ease in.
- **Confident.** All of that, plus your name in the address and one page changing through eight looks as you scroll.
- **Maximal.** All of that, plus a portfolio tile that opens out into its exhbt and "How it works" as a short film you play by scrolling. The tile can be mocked up but not built before launch, because it changes students' public pages.

No level uses 3D, mascots, confetti or anything that loops, and all of it stands still for anyone who has asked their device for less motion.

## What you'll see, and in what order

First, on Friday: the design lab, where you can flip real screens between the three levels. It has Home, the moment after publishing, a signed-in page, and one page of every button, field, menu and panel in Exhbt's style. Then, by Wednesday 2 Dec, in this order:

1. The same header and page layout everywhere.
2. The words: one name for each thing, and every step ending on what happens next.
3. The new portfolio page, already being built, with its first-minute strip.
4. The finish after publishing.
5. Home's story.

Forms, the roadmap, billing and the bigger show-off moments follow: some by Wednesday if nothing slips, the rest in the days after.

## What will not change

Students' own pages keep their looks, all eight, and every public page is checked against a saved copy before each change. The page inside the exhbt editor, which you like, stays as it is. So do the colours, the fonts, prices, addresses, links and everyone's saved work.

## Home's story, in seven steps

1. **A website of your own, built from your projects.** For students and graduates: each project becomes an exhbt, a short page an employer reads in a minute and can check. *Shown: a real portfolio, in the first screen.*
2. **One project, one page.** The question, how you did it, what you found, the file behind it. *Shown: the example exhbt.*
3. **A CV says it. An exhbt shows it.** A CV or a LinkedIn line names the skill. An exhbt is the report's one-minute version, with the PDF a click behind. *Shown: a CV line beside that exhbt's graph.*
4. **Start with the file you already have.** We draft from your files and nothing else; you check every word. *Shown: the steps as real screens.*
5. **An address that's yours.** One link for your CV, LinkedIn and email signature. *Shown: type a name and the address takes it.*
6. **Not sure what to make? Build your roadmap.** Free, no account: what the job asks for, where you stand, projects that close the gaps. *Shown: the radar.*
7. **Free to start. Pay to put it online.** The plans and the questions.

## Where we expect people to stall

There's no usage data yet, so these come from walking the journey.

- **Straight after signing up,** they land on an account page with nothing of theirs on it. *Fix:* they land on their own page, name filled in, with a big "Your best project goes here" panel and one button.
- **Waiting for the first draft,** they watch a grey bar for about twenty seconds, then get a page with no word on what was written or what to check. *Fix:* an outline of their exhbt fills in while they wait, then one line: "Here's your first draft. We wrote it from your files and nothing else. Check each number, then publish."
- **At publishing,** a paid plan ends on a link and four buttons, and Free stops at "preview only". *Fix:* the finish above; on Free we say before they start that Free keeps it private, and show them their page with the draft on it before the upgrade.

## Drawing people in and keeping them going

What we'd use:

- **Something real before we ask for anything.** The roadmap already comes before the email.
- **A head start.** They arrive to their own page with their name on it and the first step ticked, because it's true.
- **A space that asks to be filled:** the empty best-project panel, drawn full size.
- **Progress they can see,** counted from real data only.
- **A finish with one obvious next step:** "Add your next exhbt".
- **Sharing at the proudest moment:** the link as it will look, Copy, and where to put it.
- **One useful email,** with their link, the first time they publish.
- **A true reason to come back:** how often their page was opened. Later, because it must be built honestly.
- **Counting it:** how many people publish a first exhbt, and how many a second.

What we won't use:

- **Countdowns and "only 3 left".** They'd be untrue.
- **Streaks and guilt.** A job hunt isn't daily, they'd punish exam weeks, and universities would read them as spam.
- **Made-up or rounded-up numbers,** such as "12 recruiters viewed you". One caught, and employers stop trusting every exhbt.
- **Hiding cancel, unsubscribe or delete.** If leaving is hard, people stop trusting the rest.
- **Breaking links people have already sent, to make them pay again.** See question 4.

## The honest limits

- Astryx gives consistency, not character: left alone it would make the site plainer. The character is the six things above.
- Nothing has been seen on a real iPhone. Every phone picture came from a laptop pretending to be one.
- Astryx is still in beta, so each part is tried in the browser before we build on it.
- The methods above are borrowed from other products and from research we couldn't fully check; they're unproven for Exhbt until we count.
- If the new header isn't solid by Sunday night it waits, and the old one stays everywhere. Half-changed would look worse than either.

## Six questions

Each has what happens if you say nothing.

1. **Which level do you want mocked up and built?** *Confident.*
2. **May marketing pages move as you scroll?** Today's rule says nothing moves because the page scrolled; you both liked pages that do. *Yes, on marketing pages only, one moving picture a page.*
3. **Should Free be able to put one exhbt online, with "made with exhbt" on it?** The reviews call Free's lock the biggest limit on how many people publish. *No change: Free stays private, and we say so before anyone starts.*
4. **If someone stops paying, should links they've already sent keep working?** Today, if they drop to Free, every link stops, and a dead link in a recruiter's inbox hurts trust in every exhbt. *No change before 2 Dec; we decide straight after.*
5. **Home's main button (Alex).** *"Start your portfolio", from the day the new portfolio is on for everyone; "Build your exhbt" until then.*
6. **"Worked on this with other people?"** is the first thing someone reads when their first draft arrives. *It moves into the editor's menu; a real request to join still shows.*

Mock-ups come next: the direction on three real screens in the design lab (Home's first screen, the moment after publishing, a signed-in page in its new layout), built for real and looked at on a laptop and a phone, for you to choose from before anything is rebuilt.
